Curb Appeal · design exploration · demo-day edition

Three ways to make a stranger
trust a map pin.

An accessibility map lives or dies on one bet: a disabled person spends their afternoon on the strength of a pin. Every direction below encodes the same fixed product model — a four-rung trust ladder that only climbs, feature chips that name what a photo shows (never a legal claim), and a "my needs" lens — but argues for trust with a completely different voice.

01 · Field Notes 02 · Provenance 03 · Open Doors

Fixed trust ladder in all directions — AI-estimated → community-scanned → owner-verified → auditor-verified. Pins only ever go up. The lowest rung reads as informational (soft blue, dotted, italic "i"), never as a bad grade. Every tier is double-encoded: shape + mark + color, so it survives every kind of colorblindness and a grayscale projector.

Direction 01 · Editorial / warm-human

Field Notes — trust because a person stood here

The trust argument: strangers trust witnesses, not databases. This direction styles every data point as a first-hand observation from someone who physically stood at the door — the card reads like a naturalist's log ("Ramp visible — seen Tuesday by 4 neighbors"), the pins look like ink stamps pressed by a hand, and the auditor's mark sits at the top like a signature on a letter. The warmth is the argument: cold data can be wrong at you; a neighbor's note feels accountable.

Direction 02 · Precise / clinical trust

Provenance — trust because you can audit it

The trust argument: strangers trust systems that show their work. Every claim here carries its evidence chain — who observed it, when, at what confidence — rendered like a security product's certificate view. The tier ladder is a luminance ladder: more verification literally means more light, so the hierarchy survives grayscale, every colorblindness, and a washed-out projector. Nothing decorates; everything testifies.

Direction 03 · Playful / vibrant civic-tech

Open Doors — trust because everyone can see everyone helping

The trust argument: strangers trust crowds they can see. This direction makes verification a visible public act — pins physically grow as more people confirm them, so the map itself is a live scoreboard of neighborly effort ("1,204 scans on this block"). The lowest tier is a small friendly balloon, not a warning; the audited tier is the biggest sticker on the board. Energy is the argument: a map this alive is clearly being tended.

Type

Bricolage Grotesque 800 for names & numbers that celebrate · DM Sans for everything functional. Navy-on-sky-paper ≈ 13.8:1; size is the tier channel, so color never works alone.

Motion notes

  • Pins bob idle ±2px (3s sine, staggered) — the map breathes. Disabled under reduced-motion.
  • Tier upgrade: balloon inflates to the next size with a squash-and-stretch pop + one ring burst — a tiny public celebration.
  • Live scan (described): shutter → 7s balloon inflating with the % → checklist stickers slap on → pin bounces onto the map twice, block counter ticks +1.
  • Confidence rings sweep from 0 on card open, 500ms staggered.

Self-audit (all three directions)

  • Projector test: tier marks ≥34px with heavy silhouettes; B's ladder is luminance-based and C's is size-based, so both survive a washed-out projector; A's stamps rely on shape contrast on pale linen.
  • Trust smell test: every chip states what was seen, when, by whom, at what confidence — never a legal claim; the unknown state invites ("be the first to scan") instead of grading.
  • Accessibility of the palettes themselves: body text 12.9–15.4:1; every tier and match state is double-encoded (shape + mark + line-style + color, plus size in C and luminance in B), so deutan/protan/tritan and grayscale all keep the hierarchy. Halos use solid/dashed/dotted line styles, not color alone.
  • Reduced motion: all animation collapses to end-states under prefers-reduced-motion.
