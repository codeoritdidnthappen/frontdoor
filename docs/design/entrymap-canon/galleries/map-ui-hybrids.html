Curb Appeal · round two · A + C hybrids

Field Notes × Open Doors,
three genuinely different marriages.

The brief after review: keep Field Notes' warmth and witness language, keep Open Doors' visible energy — and lock the product model to three tiers. Every hybrid below encodes the same rules: Estimated (dashed, hollow, soft blue — early info, never a bad grade), Scanned on-site (the trust workhorse: photo receipts, dates, scanner counts), Owner-confirmed (attestation, storefront + outlined check — never a shield, never "verified"). Every badge opens its receipt in one tap. Freshness ages in muted amber and talks about time, not the place. The only filter is your needs — trust tier is context on the pin, never a facet.

H1 · Hearth Ledger H2 · The Willow Bulletin H3 · Dusk Shift

Fixed ladder in all three — Estimated → Scanned on-site → Owner-confirmed. Pins only climb. Triple-encoded everywhere: shape/size + mark (italic i / camera / check-with-storefront) + line style (dashed / solid / double-rule), with color as the fourth, redundant channel. Each hybrid's live-scan moment is a working mock — press the button.

Hybrid 01 · warm crowd · A materials + C growth

Hearth Ledger — the scrapbook that swells with neighbors

Trust argument: a stranger believes a ledger kept by hand — and believes it more when they can see it being kept. This hybrid sets Open Doors' growing pins and live counters in Field Notes' linen-and-espresso material: every pin is a pressed stamp, and each verification adds wax to the seal, so a bigger mark literally means more people stood at that door. The counter in the map's title is the town's pulse, ticking in serif italics like a ledger line being written.

Stage argument: at projector distance you see a warm parchment map where stamps visibly swell and a number ticks upward mid-demo. Growth reads at 30 feet; the serif warmth reads as "made by people" even before anyone can read a word.

Hybrid 02 · civic print · C palette + A editorial discipline

The Willow Bulletin — a civic poster that testifies

Trust argument: a stranger believes print. This hybrid takes Open Doors' sky-paper, navy, marigold and violet and sets them with Field Notes' editorial discipline — double-rule borders, figure numbers, and witness testimony promoted to the pull quote. Pins are fixed-size print marks whose ink weight climbs with the tier (dashed sketch → solid marigold plate → cobalt double-rule), because in print, certainty is set heavier, not louder. Every claim is captioned like a photograph in a newspaper: what was seen, when, by whom.

Stage argument: on a projector this is the highest-contrast frame of the three — navy rules on near-white, marigold plates burning at 30 feet. The demo beat is typographic: the scan "goes to press," lines typeset themselves, and the mark is stamped down onto the page like a plate hitting paper.

Hybrid 03 · my blend · adaptive warmth, sketch → lantern pins

Dusk Shift — daylight civic, lantern-lit by night

Trust argument (the thesis): trust is needed at two different hours. At 2 pm you're planning from a sofa — you want Open Doors' bright, legible civic energy. At 8 pm you're on the curb deciding whether to push down the block — you want Field Notes' warmth telling you a person was here recently. So the interface shifts with the day: one product, one grammar, two temperatures. The pin grammar carries it — Estimated is a chalk sketch (an outline of a place, honestly unfinished), Scanned on-site inflates into a marigold lantern, Owner-confirmed adds the storefront seal — and at dusk the lanterns light, so the glow on the map is literally the evidence: lit means someone stood here, unlit sketch means not yet. Glow is a fourth, redundant channel — size, mark and line-style carry the tiers on their own.

Stage argument: one button flips the whole room. Mid-demo, the map slides from daylight paper to a deep dusk where every scanned door glows like a shop window — the audience watches trust become visible light. No other direction has a single-gesture transformation this legible from the back row.

Motion — light behaves like light

  • The day↔dusk shift is one 800ms cross-tween of custom properties — nothing moves, everything warms.
  • From C: lanterns inflate with one soft overshoot; from A: sketches are over-drawn, never erased.
  • Glow is additive and slow (400ms bloom) and is never the only signal — grayscale keeps size + mark + line style.
  • Reduced motion: theme swaps instantly, lanterns appear lit, no blooms or ripples.

Self-audit — all three hybrids

  • WCAG contrast: body text 10.4–13.8:1 in every theme; the weakest text pairing (estimated blue #38678C / #20679E on light grounds) holds ≥ 4.6:1; aging-amber meta text ≥ 4.8:1 and is always accompanied by the word "old", never color alone.
  • Colorblind-safe triple encoding: tier = shape/size (36→48→56px, or ink-weight in the Bulletin) + mark (italic i / camera / storefront-with-check) + line style (dashed / solid / double-rule). Match = halo line style (solid/dashed/dotted) + tag glyph (✓/~/?) + color. Deutan, protan, tritan and full grayscale all keep both hierarchies.
  • Grayscale projector: Hearth Ledger and Dusk Shift carry tiers by size; the Bulletin by ink density; all pins ≥36px with heavy silhouettes. Dusk glow is redundant — losing it loses nothing.
  • Three tiers, fixed words: "Estimated" is dashed, hollow, soft blue, checkless — early info, never a warning; "Scanned on-site" carries the evidence budget (photo receipts, dates, scanner counts, testimony); "Owner-confirmed" is an attestation mark — storefront + outlined check, no shields, and the word "verified" appears nowhere. The cut auditor tier survives only as the Bulletin's explicitly-roadmap footnote.
  • Receipts: every tier badge is a button; one tap opens photo, date, method, observer count. Chips name features only — what a photo shows, never a legal claim.
  • Freshness: muted amber, worded about time ("newest photo is 6 weeks old"), never red, never about the place.
  • Needs, not tiers, filter: the only lens is "my needs" — good/partial/unknown halos double-encoded (line style + glyph + color). No control anywhere filters by trust tier.
  • prefers-reduced-motion: every animation — develops, presses, kindles, counters, ripples — collapses to its end state; the scan mocks jump straight to "published".
