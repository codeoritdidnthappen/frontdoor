Map ground — depth and colour

Four grounds for the same three downtown Austin blocks, against the ground the build ships today. Same projection, same sixty-four places, same pin hierarchy on top, same footprint geometry — the variants differ only in tone, light and landcover, so they can be judged against each other rather than against four different maps. Every marker is live: tap one to select it.

The rule that shapes all four

Blue is estimated, marigold is scanned on-site, purple is owner-confirmed, green is needs-match, amber is ageing evidence. Those meanings live on markers and are load-bearing. The ground may echo the whole palette, but only at a saturation and value where it reads as terrain. Concretely, enforced and measured below: no ground tone exceeds chroma 43 while the least saturated marker colour is chroma 95; no ground element uses a semantic hue at full strength; no ground element is a lone disc or a teardrop; and canopy is suppressed within 26 px of any full pin so nothing decorative lands inside a marker's halo. No red anywhere on any of them.

The measured floor, before any colour was chosen

The estimated marker is the binding constraint, because it is the only one of the three that touches raw ground: levels 1 and 2 sit on a white lift halo, level 3 is a bare dashed ring with no halo at all. Its edge is --blue-edge #3F79DC, relative luminance 0.197. For that ring to hold 3:1, every ground tone a ring can land on must have relative luminance ≥ 0.690 — about #D8D8D8 in neutral terms. That is the ceiling on how dark this map is allowed to get, and it is why all four variants are light. Depth here had to come from small tonal steps applied consistently, not from a darker palette.

And the ground we ship today already breaks it. The tree dots are #AFC2F2 (L 0.541) and #C4D0F6 (L 0.635). An estimated ring landing on one measures 2.37:1 and 2.75:1. On this dataset that happens — the dots are scattered at random across the blocks the rings sit in. Every variant below fixes it; that fix is the single most valuable thing on this page.

Two labels added, four alleys invented. The 13 px line at lat 30.26320 and the 15 px line at lng −97.74430 were unlabelled; by position in the Austin grid they are W Cesar Chavez St and Congress Ave, and both sit far enough inside the frame to carry a label. The gap between Cesar Chavez and W 2nd is 0.0018°, exactly twice every other block, so something bisects it: I put a service alley there. Three more alleys run mid-block (two east–west, one north–south between Lavaca and Colorado). Downtown Austin blocks really are alley-bisected, but these four are placed by grid arithmetic, not surveyed — they are the "thinner again" tier the spec asks for, and if the real alley positions matter they should come from data.
