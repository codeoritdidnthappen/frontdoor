Tagline gallery — “Access starts before you arrive.”

Four typographic treatments of the welcome screen’s headline, drawn on the real card, on the real aubergine hero, with the three trust-tier tiles in place beneath. The sentence does not change. Every colour is one of the canonical twelve from asset-library/tokens/variables.css or one of the prototype’s own documented mixes of two of them, and the only faces are the two the build already loads.

Read the four treatments as four answers to one question the product owner asked: “this phrase needs more enhanced ui. like the before and access are important. maybe change the font of before or change font of access. or colors?” The colour half of that question has an answer, and it is the first section of this page rather than the last, because it removes a tool rather than adding one.

The constraint: colour is spoken for on this screen

The rule, stated before anything is drawn. In this product colour carries meaning and only meaning. Three accents are owned outright by the three trust tiers — sky for Estimated, marigold for Scanned on-site, violet for Owner-confirmed — and on this screen the three tier tiles sit immediately below the headline: measured on the cards below, 95 px from the bottom of the headline to the top of the tiles, on the same white surface, with nothing between them but one line of subhead. A tier accent in the headline is therefore not a decorative choice; it is a pointer at the tile underneath it.

This is testable rather than assertable, so it is tested. Below, the headline is drawn four times at the default text size: once as the build draws it today, and three times with one word carried into a tier ink. The inks used are not approximations of the tier accents — they are the exact tokens .wl-tier.t-est b, .wl-tier.t-scan b and .wl-tier.t-own b already paint the tile labels with, so the headline word and the tile label below it are byte-identical hexes on the same surface.

One nuance worth recording, because it is the reason the four treatments below are not colourless. --ink (indigo900 #1E1142) is the product’s default text ink on every screen — it is also --match-good and the map’s label ink, but it makes no tier claim because it is what all text is. --sub (#463B63) is the same indigo carried 17.6% toward white and is the ink of the subhead directly below the headline; it is a step on the neutral ramp, not an accent. Those two, and the absence of every other ink, are the whole colour budget here.

What that leaves, and the harness

With hue unavailable, the honest tools are weight, size, italic and spacing — and one structural tool, the line break. That is not a workaround; it is the finding. A headline that has to sit above a colour legend is a headline that has to earn its emphasis from the type itself.

Each treatment is drawn three times, at the three text-size steps the app actually ships: --root-fs 15 px (Default), 16.2 px (Large) and 18.9 px (Larger). The steps are multiples of that one anchor exactly as in the prototype, so --fs-h1 is 22.5 / 24.3 / 28.35 px and every fragment size below is a multiple of it. The phone is 390 px with an 11 px bezel under border-box, so the screen is 368 px and the card’s text column is 320 px — the real measure this sentence has to wrap inside. The frame is cropped in height (668 px, not 844) so twelve of them fit on one page: the card and everything in it — padding, headline, subhead, tiles, buttons — is at full size and is what a phone renders, and the crop is absorbed entirely by the hero above, which is why the skyline sits closer to the card here than it does on a real handset.

Every ink on this page, measured

Contrast is computed in the page, from the hexes the page actually paints, by the WCAG 2.x relative luminance formula. The two surfaces are the card (--card #FFFFFF) and the tier tile (--wash-quiet #F9F7FD).

One small disagreement, recorded rather than smoothed over. phone-app-prototype.html comments --blue-ink and --marigold-ink as “8.89:1 on white”. Computed here from the shipped hexes themselves, they are 8.91:1 and 8.87:1 — the two inks were solved to a target of 8.89 and the solutions land a hundredth either side of it. Nothing is wrong with either figure and nothing depends on the difference; it is noted so that a later reader does not treat this page and the build as contradicting each other. --purple-ink agrees exactly at 11.16:1.
