Estimated mark gallery 3 — the ≈ question

Round 8, brief §1, third drawing pass. The owner asked: why not something like ≈ instead of the i? Gallery 1’s candidate B tried it once and the drawer judged that it read as water at small sizes. This page treats that as a drawing problem, not a verdict: four ways to put the “approximately” sign on the Estimated tier, each drawn so that it cannot be a river.

Every colour is one of the canonical twelve from asset-library/tokens/variables.css. Pins are composed the way pinSVG composes a library asset: the asset drawing, under it the build’s white lift strokes, under those the match halo (r 50 on the head centre, lavender disc in a match state only). No confidence dots on anything; the owner removed them. No dash or dotted stroke on any mark; the halos own those. Outline is sky700 #1B6599 throughout. Scanned and Owner are drawn as the build draws them, minus their dots.

Why B read as water, and what changes here

Cartographic water is two or three thin lines, each a wave and a half or more, evenly spaced across the shape, with a generous amplitude. The mathematical ≈ is the opposite on every axis: exactly one period per line, the two lines stacked close (about two and a half stroke widths apart), the pair much wider than it is tall, and the stroke heavy relative to the wave. Gallery 1’s B was drawn at stroke 5.5 with an 8-unit control offset and a 15-unit gap, which put it halfway to the water convention. Every ≈ on this page uses one period, a 5.5-unit control offset (peak about 4 units, wavelength-to-amplitude near 10:1), the outline’s own stroke weight (4), round caps, and a gap of 11 units between the two lines.
