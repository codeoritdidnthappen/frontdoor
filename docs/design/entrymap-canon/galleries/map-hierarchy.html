Map hierarchy and density

Sixty-four real places across three downtown blocks — the same dataset the prototype uses (52 estimated from a street-imagery run, 11 on-site scans, 1 owner-confirmed), on the same projection and the same map ground. Every panel below draws all sixty-four. They differ only in how much visual weight each one is allowed to take.

The one rule the hierarchy encodes. Pin weight says how much evidence exists about an entrance — nothing else. A bigger pin means somebody stood at that door, or the owner attested to it. It never means the entrance is better, more accessible, or recommended. Nothing on this page ever marks a place as failing: places that do not match a needs profile recede in opacity, because we lack evidence about them, and recession is not a verdict.

Density rules, running on the real data

The three rules from section 10 are implemented, not described. Move the controls and watch them bite. The tally under the map always reconciles to 64 — a place is either a marker or a member of a numbered cluster, and nothing is ever dropped.

Defaults: overlap threshold 40%, ceiling 12 full pins, estimated minimum spacing 22px.
Overlap threshold — the weaker level yields40%
Two markers are compared by true circle-intersection area over the smaller marker's area. Past the threshold the weaker level yields — estimated before scanned, scanned before owner — and drops into a cluster. Owner never yields to anything.
Ceiling on full pins at phone width12
Past the ceiling the lowest level furthest from the map centre converts to a cluster first. The real data holds exactly 12 full pins, so the default sits right on the line — drag it down to 5 and watch scanned pins become counts while the single owner pin never does.
Estimated ring minimum spacing22px
My call: yes, they need one. Drag to 0 to see the rash — rings clumping into shapes the eye reads as objects. 22px (about 1.5 ring diameters) keeps them reading as evenly-scattered texture.
Turning the hierarchy off with density still on shows how much of the calm comes from the levels rather than from the clustering.
Needs profile
Matching places hold full opacity and take a match ring. Non-matching recede in opacity only — no cross, no grey-out, no "does not meet". Most estimated places recede under any profile for the honest reason that nobody has checked them.
Selection
The selected place is exempt from every rule above: never clustered, never dimmed, never yields, always drawn on top. Tap any marker on any map to select it.
